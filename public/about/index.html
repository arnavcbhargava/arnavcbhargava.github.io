"Learning from Graph Circuit Lower Bounds" Supervised by Bruno Cavalar and Rahul Santhanam

My master's thesis, supervised by Bruno Cavalar and Rahul Santhanam, studies pseudorandomness for graph circuits (FORMULA ∘ G, where G consists of one-sided communication-cost 1 functions, studied in [Jukna12] and [Tal16], for example) and its connection to meta-complexity through the CIKK framework. The main contributions are:

  • Natural Properties from Graph Circuit Lower Bounds: Using known lower bounds (Lokam ’09), I extracted a natural property useful against graph circuits. A sign-rank lower bound implies a depth-3 graph circuit lower bound. Via Forster ’02, sign-rank can be lower bounded through the spectral norm, which is easy to compute even though sign-rank itself is NP-hard.
  • PRGs Based on Graph Circuit Hardness: I constructed new combinatorial designs that exploit the structure of graph circuits, with both probabilistic and constructive existence proofs, generalising the greedy and polynomial-over-finite-fields constructions. Adapting the Nisan–Wigderson reconstruction argument gives a graph circuit hardness-based generator with low truth-table complexity, as CIKK requires. I also showed that the stretch is tight for this method.
  • Implications for MCSP: I formalised the search-to-decision reduction implied by the CIKK framework, differently from Hirahara ’18.

“Towards Better Approximation Algorithms for α-EFX Allocations” Supervised by Aris Filos-Ratsikas
  • Exploration of the “Top-N” Case: Provided strong evidence that the Top-N instance class — where agents value only their top few goods — cannot be handled organically by the existing 3PA algorithm (Amanatidis, Filos-Ratsikas, Sgouritsa, 2024), countering an existing conjecture.
  • Attempt to Extend 2/3-EFX Guarantee from 7 to 9 Agents: Extended the proof framework of AFS (2024) to the case of 9 agents. Though not fully complete, the dissertation covers many new subcases and offers groundwork that could lead to a publishable result.
  • Use of Extremal Graph Theory (Group Champion Graphs): Suggested ways of analyzing EFX allocations using multigraphs and structural properties, inspired by work on EFX-with-charity. This adds a fresh analytical lens to the problem.
  • Empirical experiments on 3PA: Tested 3PA's running time and step execution frequency. Evaluated allocation properties under different valuation distributions. Analyzed graph-theoretic properties of allocations, including structure of “enhanced envy graphs” and critical goods.
